Garbage Disposal Repair in Round Rock, TX

We repair jammed, humming, leaking, and nonworking garbage disposals.

Call (512) 882-1291 Ask a question

In Round Rock and Brushy Creek homes, garbage disposal trouble often starts with an original kitchen unit that has handled years of food scraps. The motor, mounting ring, splash guard, and drain connection all wear differently. A disposal can also fail after a utensil, fruit pit, or glass fragment drops through the sink opening.

We check the unit beneath the kitchen sink, the electrical connection, and the discharge pipe before recommending a repair or replacement. A humming unit can sometimes wait if the sink stays unused, but water under the cabinet or a sink that will not drain should not wait because the seal, drain fitting, or cabinet base can be damaged.

Humming disposal and sink water

We hear several versions of the same call: the disposal hums but will not spin, the reset button keeps popping, or nothing happens at the switch. Metal grinding inside the chamber usually points to a trapped object or worn grinding components. A sour smell can come from food caught below the splash guard, while water beneath the sink can come from the mounting ring, dishwasher connection, or discharge pipe.

Water rising in one side of a double-bowl sink can also appear to be a disposal failure. These symptoms can look alike from above the countertop, but the motor, seal, switch, and drain connection are separate faults with different repair work and parts.

Disposal inspection and test

We start the disposal inspection by isolating power and checking the switch, reset button, cord, and wiring connections. We test the electrical supply, inspect the motor housing, and look for leaks at the sink flange and discharge elbow. Initial testing and a cabinet inspection often take 20 to 40 minutes, depending on access and the condition of the fittings.

We can usually clear a jam, remove a lodged object, reset the unit, or tighten a loose mounting assembly during the same visit. A failed motor, cracked housing, or damaged mounting system may require a replacement unit or a part matched to the existing sink opening and drain connection.

Mounting ring, shutoff, and access

The age of the disposal affects the repair path because older motor housings, mounting rings, and wiring can be worn at the same time. A simple reset or jam removal involves less work than a unit with a failed motor or a leak at the sink flange. The make and model also matter if a splash guard, mounting component, or replacement unit must be matched.

Cabinet access changes the work involved. Packed cleaning supplies, fixed shelving, a tight double-bowl sink, or a seized dishwasher shutoff can add disassembly and testing. If a specific part must be ordered, or cabinet wiring needs code-required sign-off, we explain that before the next stage of work.

Model plate and sink cabinet

Before calling, check the model number on the disposal label, usually found on the side or underside of the motor housing. Tell us which kitchen sink has the problem and whether the unit hums, leaks, or stays completely silent. Note whether water is standing in the sink and whether the dishwasher is connected to the disposal. If possible, clear space beneath the sink so we can reach the outlet, reset button, mounting ring, and drain pipe.
