Water Heater Repair in Round Rock, TX

Tank and tankless water heater repairs for leaks, cold water, and failed controls.

Call (512) 882-1291 Ask a question

A cold shower and a wet water-heater closet can both get called a broken water heater. Cold water at every hot tap points toward a heating, fuel, or control fault. A pan filling beneath the tank points toward a leak. We handle both problems in Round Rock, Brushy Creek, Windemere, and Wells Branch.

Rust-colored water, a burner that will not stay lit, or a breaker that trips can also stop hot water without a tank leak. We trace the fault from the shutoff and supply lines to the controls, heating elements, burner, or tank.

A leaking tank or relief valve should not wait because water can spread into drywall, flooring, and framing. A heater that only makes lukewarm water can sometimes wait briefly, but it still needs diagnosis before a failed element, burner, or valve leaves the home without hot water.

No hot water or banging

People call us for no hot water, water that turns cold halfway through a shower, banging or popping from the tank, and water pooling around the base. A rotten-egg smell at the hot tap, a pilot light that goes out, or a breaker that trips are different clues.

These symptoms can overlap, but sediment, a failed element, a gas control fault, a relief valve, and a tank seam are not the same repair. The needed parts and repair cost can differ widely, even when the problem sounds similar over the phone.

Tank inspection and test

We begin a tank inspection by reading the model plate and checking the cold supply, hot outlet, shutoff, drain valve, relief valve, and visible pipe joints. We read incoming water pressure at a gauge and compare outlet temperature with the thermostat setting, which is commonly around 120°F. On a tankless unit, we also check the display code, inlet screen, vent connection, and condensate line where fitted.

A diagnostic check generally takes 20 to 40 minutes. We can often replace an accessible thermostat, heating element, thermocouple, pilot assembly, or relief valve during the same visit if the matching part is available. An element replacement after draining the tank can take 45 to 90 minutes. Tank replacement, corroded pipe changes, or controls that must be ordered are scheduled for another visit.

Anode rods, shutoffs, access

The age and condition of the anode rod, gas control, elements, or tank seam affect the scope of a repair. An accessible thermostat behind a lower panel is a different job from a corroded fitting on an older tank. A unit in a tight attic, raised platform, closet, or finished utility room also takes more setup than a heater with clear floor access.

A seized shutoff can require pipe work before we can isolate the heater safely. A discontinued control, uncommon element, or tankless component may need to be ordered. If a replacement requires permit or inspection sign-off, the job also involves coordination beyond the heater itself.

Model plate and access

We can use the model number, serial number, and fuel type to identify the correct water-heater parts. On many units, the rating plate is printed on the tank jacket, near the gas control, or beside the electrical panel.

Tell us which room holds the heater and whether it is in a garage, closet, attic, or utility room. Let us know if water is running from the tank, relief valve, or nearby pipe, and whether the shutoff will turn.
