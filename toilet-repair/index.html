Toilet Repair in Round Rock, TX

We repair running, leaking, and poorly flushing toilets in Round Rock.

Call (512) 882-1291 Ask a question

A plunger is the sensible first step for a bowl that is slow to clear or has stopped flushing. It can move a soft blockage through the trap, and it costs nothing to try. The plunger stops helping if the bowl fills again, the handle does nothing, or water appears at the base.

We check the tank, bowl, supply line, and floor around the fixture to find the failed part. A toilet with ongoing water flow, a leak, or a failed flush should not wait, because it can waste water, soak flooring, or leave a bathroom without a working fixture.

Toilet keeps running

We hear about toilets that keep running after the flush, bowls that will not refill, handles that move without flushing, and water around the toilet base. A tank that hisses may have a worn fill valve, while a quiet tank that refills every few minutes may have a leaking flapper or flush-valve seal.

A weak flush can come from a low tank water level, a disconnected lift chain, or mineral buildup in the rim holes. Water on the floor may come from a supply connector, tank bolt, wax ring, or a cracked bowl. These faults can look alike from outside the fixture, but the parts and labor involved are not the same.

Fixture inspection and test

We start with a fixture inspection and test. We flush the toilet several times, check the tank water level, inspect the flapper and fill valve, and look for moisture at the supply connection, tank bolts, and base. We also check the shutoff valve and measure supply pressure, since household plumbing commonly runs between 40 and 80 psi.

A tank check often takes 15 to 30 minutes. We measure the flush-valve opening, which is commonly 2 or 3 inches, before fitting a flapper or seal. Standard flappers, fill valves, trip levers, supply connectors, and tank seals can often be replaced during the same visit. We schedule a further visit if a brand-specific part must be ordered or the toilet must be removed for a base leak.

Shutoff valves and tank parts

The age and design of the tank parts affect the work. A common flapper or fill valve is usually more straightforward than a discontinued flush valve, a concealed actuator, or a part made only for one toilet model. A tank lid or bowl that has cracked can also change the scope of the repair.

Access at the shutoff valve matters too. A seized angle stop may need replacement before we can safely work on the supply line. A leak at the base can require lifting the toilet to inspect the wax ring and floor flange, especially if the fixture rocks or the flooring has softened.

Tank model and bathroom access

Have the toilet model number available if you can find it. It is often stamped inside the tank or printed beneath the tank lid. Tell us which bathroom has the problem and whether the toilet is running, leaking, or failing to flush now.

Let us know the approximate age of the fixture and whether the shutoff valve is easy to reach behind the bowl. Clear a path to the bathroom if storage, rugs, or furniture block the fixture.
