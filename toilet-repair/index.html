Toilet Repair in Round Rock, TX

We fix running, leaking, clogged, and weak-flushing toilets in Round Rock.

Call (512) 882-1291 Ask a question

We see a plunger as the sensible first tool for a bowl that will not clear. We expect homeowners to try a few steady plunges first, because paper can lodge in the trapway and then release.

We know a plunger has a limit when the bowl refills, the water level rises, or the blockage returns after every flush. A running tank or water at the toilet base needs a different repair than a clog.

We can schedule a toilet that has cleared and is flushing normally. A toilet that keeps running, leaks onto the floor, backs up, or will not flush should not wait because water can damage flooring and leave the fixture unusable.

Running water and weak flushes

We often hear, "My toilet keeps running," after the tank has refilled but water still moves through the overflow tube. We also hear, "The toilet will not flush," or, "It flushes but the bowl stays full."

We check for a weak flush that leaves paper in the bowl, a gurgling sound after flushing, and water around the base of the fixture. A toilet can also smell musty near the floor if a seal has failed or water has been sitting below the bowl.

We treat these as separate faults, even when they sound similar over the phone. A fill valve, flapper, flush valve, wax ring, trapway blockage, and loose tank bolt each need different parts and labor.

Fixture inspection and test

We start the fixture inspection by flushing the toilet, checking the bowl level, and watching the tank refill. We inspect the flapper, fill valve, overflow tube, supply line, shutoff valve, tank bolts, and base of the bowl.

We can use dye in the tank to find a flapper leak, and the test commonly takes about 15 minutes. We also check whether the tank water sits roughly 1 inch below the top of the overflow tube and may measure supply pressure, which is commonly kept between 40 and 80 psi in residential plumbing.

We can usually replace a standard flapper, fill valve, handle, or supply line during the same visit if the fitting matches available parts. A cracked bowl, damaged flange, seized hardware, or discontinued tank component may need a return visit after the correct part is located.

Fill valves, shutoffs, and access

The age of the fill valve, flapper, and flush valve affects the repair because older tank parts may be worn, brittle, or no longer matched to current replacements. A standard tank with an accessible lid is simpler to work on than a concealed carrier or a fixture set tight against tile.

The shutoff valve also matters. If the valve beneath the tank has seized or the supply line cannot be safely disconnected, the repair can extend beyond the parts inside the tank.

We also account for corroded tank bolts, a toilet that must be lifted to reach the flange, and any part that has to be ordered by model. Property-manager approval or required sign-off can add coordination before work on the fixture moves ahead.

Tank model numbers and shutoffs

We ask you to note which bathroom has the problem and whether the toilet is running, leaking, backing up, or failing to flush now. We also ask whether water is visible at the base, under the tank, or near the shutoff valve.

We can use a model number stamped inside the tank or printed on the underside of the tank lid to help match parts. We also benefit from the approximate age of the toilet and any tight access around the bowl or tank.
