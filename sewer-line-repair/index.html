Sewer Line Repair in Round Rock, TX

Camera-led sewer line repair for backups, roots, breaks, and damaged pipe.

Call (512) 882-1291 Ask a question

Most calls start with “the toilet flushes but the tub fills,” “there is a sewer smell in the yard,” or “water is coming up through the shower drain.” Those descriptions point beyond one fixture. We trace the building sewer, the pipe carrying wastewater from the house to the sewer main or septic connection.

Other callers notice a washing machine drain makes the toilet gurgle, or that several sinks drain slowly at once. A blocked, cracked, shifted, or root-filled sewer line can cause those symptoms. The camera and locator show where the trouble sits.

Sewage backing into a tub, shower, floor drain, or yard should not wait. Wastewater can keep moving through the pipe and spread into rooms or soil, making the affected section harder to repair.

Sewage in tubs and yards

Water rising in a shower after a toilet flush is a common report. So is a toilet that bubbles when the washing machine drains, a sewer odor near the cleanout, or a wet patch above the buried line. Each symptom gives us a different starting point for the inspection.

A slow sink alone may be a fixture drain issue, but several slow fixtures often point farther down the sewer pipe. A yard smell may come from a cracked joint, a loose cleanout cap, or sewage escaping from a damaged section. Gurgling can also come from a partial blockage that has not fully stopped flow.

These faults can sound alike over the phone, but they do not involve the same pipe section, equipment, or repair work. A camera inspection separates a root blockage from a broken pipe, a belly, or a failing connection before repair decisions are made.

Camera inspection and locating

We start at the cleanout where access allows. A camera head travels through the sewer pipe, and its footage shows roots, cracks, offsets, standing water, or collapsed sections. An accessible camera inspection often takes 20 to 40 minutes, depending on the pipe route and blockage.

We can use a locator above ground to mark the camera head and estimate the line route and depth. We check the cleanout, pipe material, visible joints, and the connection path toward the street or septic area. Camera work, locating, and accessible cleanout checks are usually handled during the visit; excavation, pipe replacement under a slab, permits, or required sign-off may need separate scheduling.

Pipe depth, material, access

Pipe material changes the repair approach. Clay tile can separate at joints, cast iron can corrode from the inside, and older Orangeburg pipe can deform or flatten. PVC usually has different connection and replacement requirements than those older materials.

Depth and access also affect the work. A line beneath open soil is different from a section under a driveway, foundation slab, tree roots, fencing, or landscaping. A seized cleanout, a difficult camera entry point, an ordered fitting, or municipal sign-off can add equipment, restoration, or return work to the repair.

Cleanout location and fixtures

Before calling, check which fixtures are affected: toilet, shower, tub, washing machine standpipe, or floor drain. Tell us whether sewage or water is rising now, and whether the problem started after laundry, a flush, or heavy water use.

If you can find the cleanout, note whether it is in the yard, garage, basement, or near an exterior wall. A cleanout is often a capped pipe near ground level, but its location varies by property. Let us know about gates, locked side yards, vehicles, concrete, or landscaping above the suspected pipe route.

Older home records or a prior camera report can help identify clay, cast iron, PVC, or Orangeburg pipe. Call Round Rock Plumbing Company at (512) 882-1291 before sewage reaches more fixtures, floors, or yard soil.
