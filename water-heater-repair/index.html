Water Heater Repair in Round Rock, TX

We diagnose no-hot-water, leaks, rumbling, and control faults in Round Rock.

Call (512) 882-1291 Ask a question

Cold water at the shower and water reaching the hallway from a utility closet are both called a water heater problem. They point to different faults: one can involve a burner, element, control, or gas supply; the other can come from a valve, pipe joint, relief line, or tank.

We check both tank and tankless units in Round Rock, Brushy Creek, Windemere, and Wells Branch. We trace the fault at the heater before replacing a part.

No hot water without a leak can usually wait for a planned repair. Water at the tank, a gas smell, a hot relief line, or a tripped breaker should not wait, because a failed vessel, gas connection, or electrical component can worsen quickly.

No hot water or rumbling

No hot water may mean cold water at every faucet, or it may mean a shower that turns cold after several minutes. A pilot that will not stay lit, a clicking tankless unit, and a breaker that trips while an electric heater runs point in other directions.

Brown water from the hot side, popping or rumbling under the tank, and water from the temperature-and-pressure relief pipe need separate checks. A puddle at the top fittings is not the same fault as water coming through the tank base.

These symptoms can look alike from the hallway, but they come from separate parts and lead to different repair costs. We inspect the burner, element, valve, control, and tank before identifying the work.

Tank and burner callout

On a callout, we identify the fuel or electrical supply, read the model plate, and inspect the tank, burner chamber, heating elements, thermostat, vent, relief valve, and nearby pipe joints. We measure incoming water pressure, check hot-water temperature, and test voltage or ignition where the unit uses it.

A straightforward diagnostic check commonly takes 30 to 60 minutes. A loose connection, thermostat, element, igniter, or relief valve can often be addressed on the same visit if the matching part is available.

A corroded tank seam, inaccessible component, or part that must be ordered needs a return visit. We explain whether a tank replacement should be scheduled rather than attempting a short-term patch.

Anode age, closet access, shutoffs

The age and condition of the anode rod, gas control, elements, and relief valve affect the repair scope. Older fittings may have corrosion at threads or a seized drain valve, which adds removal work.

A heater in an open garage gives us different access than one in a tight closet, attic, or finished utility room. A shutoff that will not close can require water isolation beyond the heater connections.

A common stocked part keeps the repair contained. A discontinued control, specific tankless component, permit sign-off, or ordered manufacturer part changes the visit and may require a return.

Model plate and closet access

Find the model and serial number on the rating plate before you call. On a tank, the plate is usually on the outer jacket; on a tankless unit, it is often behind or beside the front cover.

Tell us the approximate age of the unit and whether it is gas, electric, or tankless. Note which room has no hot water and whether every hot faucet is affected.

Say whether water is running from the relief pipe, drain valve, top connections, or tank base. Let us know if the heater sits in a closet, attic, garage, or another space with a narrow access route.
